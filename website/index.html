What you’ll see, step by step

Three steps. The pictures show exactly what to press: look for the 1 yellow numbers.

  1. 1

    Step 1: The download

    Press Get AurOS. Your computer saves a file called AurOS-Setup.exe.

    Your browser will probably warn you that the file “isn’t commonly downloaded”. That’s normal. Browsers say this about every program they haven’t seen many times, and free programs like AurOS are rarely seen. This is how to keep it:

    In Microsoft Edge

    1. Press … (three dots) next to the file, then Keep.
    2. Press Show more, then Keep anyway.

    In Google Chrome

    1. Press the file where it says Suspicious download blocked.
    2. Press Download suspicious file.

    In Firefox

    If it says the file is not commonly downloaded: open the list of downloads (the arrow at the top right), press the file, then press Allow download.

    When it has finished, open it: press Open file under its name, or find AurOS-Setup.exe in your Downloads folder and double-click it.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Windows asks if it’s all right

    Windows will probably show a blue box: “Windows protected your PC”. That’s normal too. Windows says this about every program it hasn’t seen many times, and it is usual for free software. Press More info, then Run anyway.

    Then Windows asks whether AurOS may make changes. Press Yes.

    Only if Windows says “Smart App Control blocked an app that may be unsafe”

    Some newer Windows 11 computers have a setting called Smart App Control. It blocks programs Windows hasn’t seen many times, and it has no Run anyway button. Nothing has been changed on your computer. Close the message, then switch that setting off:

    1. Press Start, type Windows Security, and open it.
    2. Press App & browser control.
    3. Press Smart App Control settings.
    4. Choose Off. If Windows asks you to confirm, press Yes.
    5. Open AurOS-Setup.exe again from your Downloads folder, and carry on from More info above.

    Good to know before you switch it off: on many computers, once Smart App Control is off, it can’t be switched back on without setting Windows up again from the start. Windows’ other protection, Microsoft Defender, keeps working either way.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Pick a look, press Install AurOS

    AurOS checks your computer first. Then you pick the look you like and press Install AurOS.

    AurOS gets ready while you wait. Then the computer restarts once, by itself. Leave it switched on.

    When AurOS starts, it asks whether everything works. Have a look around first if you like; it asks again next time. Until you press Yes, Windows and your files are kept just as they were. If AurOS ever fails to start, the computer starts Windows by itself, and No, go back to Windows brings Windows back.

Pick the look you want

You choose in the installer. You can change it later in Settings, under “How this computer works”.

  • Like Windows

    A bar along the bottom, with a button for each thing you have open.

  • Like a Mac

    Your favourite programs sit in a row at the bottom, always in the same place.

  • Big and simple

    A home page of big buttons for the things you do most. Everything you open sits in a row at the edge, so nothing gets lost.

  • One thing at a time

    Press a big button and that one thing fills the screen. One big Home button always brings you back.

Good to know

How long it takes
About 10 minutes on a newer computer with a solid-state drive and fast internet. An older computer with a spinning hard disk, or slow internet, takes longer.
Which computers
A Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer made after about 2013. Not Macs, Chromebooks, phones or tablets.
Room it needs
AurOS uses up to 40 GB of the empty space on your computer (less on a small drive), and always leaves Windows at least 20 GB free, more on a big drive. The installer checks this for you.
Keep it plugged in
Plug in the charger before you start, and leave it in until AurOS has started.
If AurOS can’t go on your computer
The installer checks your computer first. If something would stop AurOS working, it says so in plain words before it changes anything, and your computer stays exactly as it was.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. No adverts, no subscription, nothing to pay later. AurOS is made in the open, so anyone can check how it works.

Will I lose my files?

No. AurOS goes into empty space on your computer, and your files stay in Windows, untouched. When AurOS first starts, it offers to bring copies of your documents and pictures across. As with any big change, photos you treasure are safest with a copy somewhere else too, such as a memory stick.

Can I go back to Windows?

Yes. Until you tell AurOS that everything works, Windows and your files are kept just as they were, and No, go back to Windows brings Windows back. If AurOS ever fails to start, the computer starts Windows by itself. Later, Put Windows back in AurOS’s Settings makes the computer start Windows again, as it was.

What about my programs, like Microsoft Office?

Programs made for Windows don’t run in AurOS. AurOS comes with its own for letters, email, photos and the internet, and Get more programs has many more, free. You can use Microsoft Office in the internet browser, at office.com. If you need a Windows program, you can always go back to Windows.

Is it safe?

AurOS checks your computer before it changes anything, keeps Windows and your files, and gets its security updates by itself every day. Windows and your browser warn you about it for the same reason they warn about most free programs: they haven’t seen it many times. The steps above show what to press.
